**Multimodal INference and Data Science for Epidemiology and Treatment** (MINDSET) is the research programme led by **Dr Justin C Yang**, Senior Research Fellow in the [Division of Psychiatry](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/psychiatry) at [University College London](https://www.ucl.ac.uk).

I am a psychiatric epidemiologist studying how complex real-world data can be used to understand mental health, vulnerability, inequalities, and care across the life course. My research brings together linked administrative data, electronic health records, longitudinal and environmental data, clinical free text, and intensive measures of lived experience, using methods from epidemiology, causal inference, spatial analysis, and data science.

The central question across this work is how we can make rigorous and responsible inferences from data that capture only partial traces of people's lives. MINDSET therefore combines methodological development with substantive research on severe mental illness, neurodivergence, addiction, and health inequalities, alongside work on research infrastructure, data governance, open science, and public involvement.

## Research programme

### Mental health trajectories and inequalities

I lead research examining how social, emotional, environmental, and institutional experiences shape trajectories into and through severe mental illness. This includes **WISDOM**, my UKRI Mental Health Platform-funded programme integrating evidence across major UK mental-health research hubs and data resources.

### Administrative data and neurodivergence

My **ADR UK-funded research** uses linked health and education data from ECHILD to investigate inequalities in school attendance, exclusion, alternative provision, health outcomes, and other experiences among neurodivergent children and young people.

### Addiction treatment, services, and data

I work across epidemiology, policy, and health services research to improve understanding of addiction treatment and the data used to evaluate it. Through **UNITED**, I lead work examining how England's National Drug Treatment Monitoring System can better support research, policy, clinical practice, and service improvement.

### Clinical data and research infrastructure

I help lead the **North London NHS Foundation Trust Research Database**, supporting research using routinely collected mental-health records and developing collaborations across NHS and academic partners. My methodological interests include the use of clinical free text, natural language processing, and emerging language-model approaches in mental-health research.

## How we work

MINDSET is collaborative by design. I work with researchers, doctoral students, NHS organisations, public-sector data providers, charities, people with lived experience, and policy and research partners across the UK and internationally.

Across these collaborations, I aim to develop research that is methodologically rigorous, transparent and reproducible, attentive to inequalities, and accountable to the people and communities represented in the data.
