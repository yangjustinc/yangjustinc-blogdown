My teaching and supervision are organised around a simple aim: helping trainees become thoughtful, independent researchers who can reason clearly with complex health and population data. I teach quantitative methods, supervise research across mental health and population health, and contribute to open and reproducible research training.

Teaching & training

At UCL, I teach epidemiology, statistics, and research methods across postgraduate mental-health programmes. Current and recent teaching includes:

Core Principles of Mental Health Research
Research design, critical appraisal, and the foundations of mental-health research.
Practical Statistics for Mental Health Research
Applied quantitative reasoning and statistical analysis for mental-health research.
Epidemiological Research Methods in Mental Health
Epidemiological designs, measures, inference, and interpretation in psychiatric research.

I have also contributed to curriculum development around open science and public involvement and engagement with research. As a certified instructor with The Carpentries, I teach practical data and computational skills for open and reproducible research. Selected reusable teaching materials are available through Open Research & Resources.

Prospective trainees

I am interested in supervising PhD, MSc, and undergraduate projects that fit the wider MINDSET agenda, particularly in psychiatric epidemiology, linked administrative or clinical data, causal inference, mental-health data science, neurodivergence, addiction, and inequalities. Prospective trainees are welcome to get in touch with a brief outline of their interests and the type of project or supervision they are seeking.
