I have taught epidemiology, statistics, and research methods at UCL and Queen Mary University of London, supervised taught postgraduate research projects, and co-supervise doctoral students. I also teach reproducible data analysis to mental health researchers.

I am subsidiary supervisor to two UCL PhD students and sit on three thesis committees. As subsidiary supervisor to Vanessa Cieplinska, I chaired her successful upgrade examination (March 2026), and we co-authored written evidence to the House of Commons Education Committee. I also served on Dr Nathalie Rich’s thesis committee until she completed in 2025; she now works in my group as a postdoctoral research fellow.

Teaching

I teach, or have taught, on three MSc modules in the UCL Division of Psychiatry:

  • Core Principles of Mental Health Research
    Research design, critical appraisal, and the foundations of mental health research.
  • Practical Statistics for Mental Health Research
    Applied statistical analysis for mental health research.
  • Epidemiological Research Methods in Mental Health
    Epidemiological designs, measures, inference, and interpretation in psychiatric research.

From 2021 to 2025 I was a Visiting Lecturer at the Wolfson Institute of Population Health, Queen Mary University of London, where I supervised 17 MSc dissertations.

Research training and development

  • Reproducible data analysis. As a certified Carpentries instructor, I designed and taught a free four session course in reproducible analysis in R and Python for mental health researchers (2026), and I was invited by Datacise Open Learning to teach good practice in R to users of the ONS Secure Research Service across England (November 2026); the workshop materials are openly archived with a DOI.
  • Research Development Programme Lead, Division of Psychiatry (2023 to 2025). I used annual needs assessments to shape the Division’s training in quantitative methods, fellowship preparation, open science, public involvement, and research management.
  • Early career researchers. Since 2022 I have built and coordinated the Division’s community of non-clinical early career researchers (around 50 to 60 members), with regular fellowship development and peer feedback sessions, and have since shared its leadership with colleagues.

Reusable teaching materials are listed under Code and resources, and courses I have delivered under Talks.

Taught research projects

Since 2020 I have supervised 32 MSc and BSc research projects, 25 of them as primary supervisor. Of these, 3 led to peer reviewed papers with the student as first author, and I currently hold 5 doctoral supervision and committee roles.

My taught project supervision spans mental health, addiction, neurodivergence, climate and health, health inequalities, and health policy, at UCL and Queen Mary University of London.

Prospective doctoral students

I welcome enquiries from prospective PhD students whose questions fit my research on school and health, mental health and addiction services, or health and social care in severe mental illness, particularly projects on where needs are recognised or missed, including when they span more than one service. Please get in touch with a short outline of your question, the data or methods you have in mind, and your likely funding route.
