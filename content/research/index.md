My research asks why the identification, treatment, and outcomes of mental health, neurodevelopmental, and substance use needs differ between social groups and places. Most of it uses England's national administrative and clinical records: linked education and hospital data, NHS mental health records, and national drug and alcohol treatment data.

Records like these reflect who reaches services, how practitioners record what they see, and how datasets are linked, as well as the needs themselves. A second strand of my work is therefore about the records: what they can and cannot show, and how to make them fit for research. That work runs from analytical methods to the governance and infrastructure that research depends on.

I lead or co-lead each line of work below through a competitively funded award. See [Funding](../funding/) for award details and [Publications](../publications/) for the full record.

## Neurodivergence, education, and health

Autistic and other neurodivergent children are identified, supported, and excluded from school at very different rates depending on who they are and where they live. My work examines those differences and what follows from them.

- **What we found.** In a national study of more than 7 million pupils in English state schools, recorded autism incidence varied markedly between social and ethnic groups and between areas ([*Lancet Child & Adolescent Health*, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-4642(22)00247-4); joint first author, leading the population and spatial analyses). The OECD's 2026 report on rising childhood autism diagnoses cites it as evidence of under-diagnosis among girls from minority ethnic groups.
- **What I lead now.** HOPE-SEN, my ADR UK fellowship (2024 to 2026), used linked national education and hospital records (ECHILD) to study school absence, exclusion, alternative provision, and hospital admission among pupils receiving support for neurodevelopmental needs. Three first author papers are in preparation, and the analysis code is [openly archived](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22710433).
- **Policy and training.** With my doctoral student Vanessa Cieplinska and Professor Vasiliki Totsika, I submitted written evidence to the House of Commons Education Committee on persistent school absence among autistic pupils and pupils with intellectual disability (2026).

## Addiction treatment and services

Effective treatment for drug and alcohol problems exists, but who receives it, and how quickly, varies widely. I study that variation and the national data used to monitor it.

- **What we found.** My doctoral research used national US survey and treatment provider data to show inequalities in unmet need for mental health treatment ([*International Journal for Equity in Health*, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12939-019-1026-y)) and in the provision of opioid agonist treatment by area, organisation, and payment ([*PLOS ONE*, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229787)); I was first author on both. In England, I contributed to a cohort study of social deprivation, mental health diagnoses, and access to treatment for opioid use disorder ([*Substance Abuse Treatment, Prevention, and Policy*, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13011-023-00568-5)).
- **What I lead now.** UNITED, an NIHR Team Science award for which I am the named lead investigator, brings together researchers, the national treatment providers Change Grow Live and Turning Point, and lived experience researchers. I led its scoping review of 83 research outputs that use England's National Drug Treatment Monitoring System ([preprint](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7505399), under review), and I now lead work on principles for using those data responsibly.
- **Also.** I lead the quantitative evaluation of a co-produced anti-stigma course for health and care staff who work with people who use drugs, funded through the NIHR School for Public Health Research. I am an Associate Editor of *Addiction*.

## Severe mental illness in clinical records

NHS mental health records hold years of detail about treatment and outcomes, much of it in free text. I use them to study treatment, harms, and inequalities in severe mental illness.

- **What we found.** Among 13,391 patients in a London mental health service, antipsychotic polypharmacy was associated with endocrine and metabolic adverse drug reactions recorded in routine care ([*Psychological Medicine*, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291722000952); joint first author).
- **What I lead now.** WISDOM, my UKRI Mental Health Platform Cross-Hub Fellowship (2026 to 2029), brings together the Complex Emotions, DATAMIND, and Social Health hubs to study the social and emotional drivers of severe and enduring mental illness. I also lead the clinical records work package of a £1.5 million NIHR study of enhanced patient observation on psychiatric wards, where we use natural language processing to identify observation practice in clinical notes.
- **Collaborations.** I contribute to research on extreme heat and mental health service use, and to a Complex Emotions Hub study of the diagnosis of personality disorder.

## Making routine data fit for research

Research with sensitive records depends on how those records are governed, linked, accessed, and documented. I treat that as part of the science.

- **Research database leadership.** Since 2019 I have helped lead the research database of what is now North London NHS Foundation Trust, first as Database Manager and now as Deputy Lead. After the merger of Camden and Islington and Barnet, Enfield and Haringey mental health trusts, I led the integration of their records into a single research database: coordinating technical teams and the data processor, renewing ethics approval, securing Confidentiality Advisory Group support, and revising access arrangements. I have advised on 31 research projects using the database, and I work on harmonisation with the equivalent databases at South London and Maudsley, Oxford Health, and Cambridgeshire and Peterborough.
- **National data governance.** I am an Expert Member of the Health Research Authority's Confidentiality Advisory Group, which advises on the use of confidential patient information without consent, and I sit on the Eastern England Secure Data Environment's Data Access Committee and an ADR UK Task and Finish Group on researchers' access to data.
- **Public involvement and linkage.** I co-led an HDR UK and DARE UK project embedding national public involvement standards in the research database's governance, and I co-lead a work package on the acceptability and governance of linking health and social care data in a new NIHR School for Social Care Research study.

## Methods

I work mainly with:

- linked national administrative data, including education, hospital, and treatment records, in trusted research environments
- electronic health records and natural language processing of clinical text, including large language models used within governed environments
- longitudinal, multilevel, fixed effects, and spatial models
- causal inference for observational data
- audits of how research collects and reports data on under-represented groups, which I designed as senior author for alcohol research ([*LGBT Health*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1089/lgbt.2023.0085)) and which our team has since applied to cardiovascular research

I release analysis code and teaching materials openly where data cannot be shared; see [Code and resources](../resources/).
