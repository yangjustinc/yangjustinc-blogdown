The contribution of research extends beyond publications and grants. Much of my work involves **creating the conditions in which useful, trustworthy research can happen**: improving access to complex data, contributing to research governance, connecting academic and service perspectives, developing researchers, and helping shape how evidence is produced and used.

I distinguish these enabling and engagement activities from downstream impact. Where there is clear evidence that research has changed policy, practice, infrastructure, or the work of others, I aim to describe that specifically. Where the contribution is to create capacity, provide independent expertise, or improve the research environment, I describe it on those terms rather than treating all activity as impact.

## Enabling research with clinical and administrative data

As **Deputy Lead of the North London NHS Foundation Trust Research Database**, I help enable ethically governed research using routinely collected mental-health records. My work spans research development, data governance, methodological support, and collaboration between NHS clinicians, informatics teams, academic researchers, and public contributors.

I also work with colleagues at South London and Maudsley NHS Foundation Trust on approaches to harmonisation across mental-health research databases. The aim is to make cross-site replication, comparative studies, and collaborative research easier to undertake while respecting the governance and technical requirements of individual NHS organisations.

Across my wider work with linked administrative and clinical data, I contribute to the development of research practices that recognise that routinely collected data are produced through institutions and therefore need careful interpretation, transparent methods, and appropriate governance.

They complement my formal academic leadership and peer-review roles, which are described on the [Leadership & Recognition](../leadership/) page.

## Working with services, policy, and public partners

Within **UNITED**, I work with treatment providers, researchers, people with lived experience, and other partners to examine how England's National Drug Treatment Monitoring System can better support research, policy, clinical practice, and service improvement. My contribution includes leading work on principles for good practice in the use and interpretation of routinely collected addiction-treatment data.

More broadly, I work with NHS organisations, public-sector data providers, charities, and policy and research partners to develop questions that are analytically credible and relevant beyond academia. This includes attention to how evidence is interpreted, what routinely collected data can and cannot represent, and how people whose experiences are contained within those data are involved in decisions about their use.

## Developing researchers and research capacity

Researcher development is another important part of my contribution. I supervise doctoral, master's, and undergraduate research and provide quantitative and methodological teaching in psychiatric epidemiology, causal inference, health-data science, and open and reproducible research.

I am a certified instructor with [The Carpentries](https://carpentries.org/) and contribute to methods training and researcher development within UCL and through collaborative research. Selected reusable materials are available through [Open Research & Resources](../resources/), while my current supervision and teaching are described in more detail on the [Teaching & Supervision](../teaching/) page.

## Open and reproducible research

Where research code, methods, or teaching materials can be shared responsibly, I aim to make them reusable rather than treating them as disposable outputs of individual projects. This includes public teaching resources, documented code, reproducible analytical workflows, and the infrastructure used to maintain this website.

Not all work using sensitive administrative or clinical data can be made public. In those settings, reproducibility also depends on clear analytical documentation, versioned code, structured workflows, and research practices that can be transferred into secure environments.

## From contribution to impact

Some of this work is intended to produce longer-term changes in research infrastructure, data use, policy, services, or research culture. Those effects often emerge over longer timescales than individual studies or grants.

Rather than equating activity with impact, I aim to document concrete downstream changes when they can be evidenced. This page therefore focuses on the contributions and forms of engagement that support those pathways, and can evolve as stronger evidence of uptake, use, or change becomes available.
