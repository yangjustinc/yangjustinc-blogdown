I hold editorial, advisory, research-assessment, infrastructure, and institutional roles across addiction, mental health, health-data research, and research governance.

MINDSET programme leadership

I established and lead MINDSET to understand how mental health develops across lives, places, and systems by integrating heterogeneous evidence across levels, settings, and timescales. Inference is the programme’s organising idea: substantive questions determine which measurements and methods are needed, while research infrastructure enables that work. Its current empirical foundations span linked administrative and clinical data, longitudinal and environmental evidence, and intensive lived-experience measures; wider collaborations contribute complementary behavioural and biological expertise. I set its intellectual and methodological direction, develop collaborations and funding, and build the data, governance, and training relationships needed to deliver the work.

MINDSET is deliberately collaborative. Programme leadership means maintaining a coherent long-term agenda while enabling researchers, trainees, partner organisations, and people with lived experience to shape its questions, methods, interpretation, and practical use.
